        Understanding AI Quant Scores

        The Golden Rule: The ideal buying opportunity comes at the golden confluence of an undervalued stock with high liquidity, low volatility, and favorable technicals.

        1. Valuation Score

        This score measures how cheap or expensive a stock is relative to its fundamentals. A low score (e.g., undervalued) suggests the market may not be fully pricing in the company's true worth, offering a potential margin of safety. It's calculated using traditional metrics like PE (Price-to-Earnings), PB (Price-to-Book), EV/EBITDA, and Market Cap to Sales ratios.

        2. Technical Score

        This score analyzes short to medium-term price trends and momentum. A low score (e.g., oversold) indicates that the stock might be due for a bounce or extended upward movement based on recent trading activity. It incorporates indicators like the RSI (Relative Strength Index) and moving averages (EMA crossovers).

        3. Liquidity Score

        Liquidity defines how easily you can buy or sell shares without causing a significant impact on the stock price. High liquidity means tighter bid-ask spreads and higher trading volume, which reduces trading costs and execution risks. It is calculated analyzing overall trading volumes and implied bid-ask spreads. It typically highlights investor interest in the stock. More the interest, most is typically the buying interest for undervalued stocks.

        4. Volatility Score

        Volatility measures the magnitude of price fluctuations over time. A lower volatility score indicates a more stable stock price, meaning less risk of sudden deep drawdowns. It considers metrics like Beta, Implied Volatility (IV), Historical Volatility (HV), Average True Range (ATR), and market-wide VIX.
